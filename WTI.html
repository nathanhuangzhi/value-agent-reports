Business & market dynamics: Production from high‑liquids Gulf of Mexico assets is tracking at the top of guidance, helped by stronger realized prices. Yet the environment is mixed: a planned facility turnaround is pushing lease operating expenses higher, and an unresolved litigation overhang injects uncertainty. The emphasis on low‑capital workovers and recompletions – rather than new drilling – signals a maintenance‑oriented posture designed to sustain output through a still‑volatile commodity cycle.

Management strategy and execution: Management has pivoted sharply toward financial discipline, slashing capital spending and concentrating on workovers, recompletions, and plug‑and‑abandonment activity to hold production near current levels. Balance‑sheet strengthening has been a priority; a refinancing with new second‑lien notes and a revolver, an asset sale, and an insurance recovery have lowered net debt and improved liquidity, while a nominal dividend reflects cautious shareholder returns. Improved flexibility is evident, though managing rising costs and litigation resolution remain key tests of execution.
